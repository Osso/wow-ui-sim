## Safety scope and observation limits

The source and saved run support narrowly scoped, recorded-owning-cgroup pidfd signaling, including executed threshold handling. They do not establish a general kernel-level isolation guarantee. Enumeration, pidfd acquisition, numeric-PID /proc membership lookup, and signal delivery are separate operations; membership checks are not atomic with signaling, and migration/PID-reuse races were not exercised or ruled out. Missing-process handling is explicit; membership assertion or other errors stop the watcher and write pressure-watch-error.json. No kernel behavior was tested.

The two-second SIGKILL stage has no recorded targets in this run; its nonempty-target behavior was not observed. JSONL records cgroup presence rather than PID membership snapshots; the signal result records successful send calls, not independently observed per-process termination times. Sampling has finite granularity and ends at the threshold; it is not a complete pressure history. Current source inspection is not an immutable hash/provenance proof of the exact code executed earlier.

No causal pressure attribution: worker MemoryPeak=2400354304 bytes is below its configured 5G MemoryHigh. The measured PSI is host-global; other-workload context is unknown. Neither that comparison nor the threshold abort proves the worker caused, or did not contribute to, host pressure. Cgroup removal likewise does not prove host-wide pressure recovery.
